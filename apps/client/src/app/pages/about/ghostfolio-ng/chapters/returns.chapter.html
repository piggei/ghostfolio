Financial Performance and Returns

A monetary gain and a percentage return answer different questions. Always compare the same date range, currency, portfolio scope and gross/net basis. These methods reuse your recorded activities and available prices; missing data or incorrect activity dates cannot be repaired by switching the method.

Open Settings, enable Experimental Features and choose Performance Calculation: ROAI, ROI, TWR or MWR. The selector is unavailable while impersonating another user or without permission to change settings. Review Home and Analysis after changing it. This guide explains the NG implementations; some methods and controls are experimental.

ROI — Return on Investment

ROI divides net investment performance by invested capital, without time weighting. Net performance includes realized and unrealized investment gains and income, less applicable investment fees and taxes. At portfolio level the denominator accumulates positive net changes in remaining invested cost; a sale does not subtract from capital already counted. Same-day internal reinvestment is netted, and capital invested and released intraday is included through the daily peak adjustment. A holding’s denominator instead uses its buy amounts.

For a selected date range, ROI starts with the opening market value and adds positive subsequent net investment changes and the relevant intraday adjustments. Reinvestment on different dates can count capital again. It is not simply gain divided by your latest bank balance, nor by all cash deposits.

ROAI — Return on Average Investment

ROAI divides net monetary investment performance by average invested capital. For a holding’s whole history, remaining invested cost is weighted by elapsed calendar intervals between investment activities and the terminal valuation. The portfolio sums the included holdings’ averages. For a selected date range, the calculator includes opening unrealized performance in the opening capital and counts the final valuation day in the daily average. These conventions can produce different averages in whole-history holding statistics and date-range charts. Calendar-day weighting avoids daylight-saving distortions. ROAI is neither TWR nor an annualized XIRR.

TWR — Time-Weighted Return

TWR geometrically links daily growth factors. When the preceding value is positive, the factor is (current value − net investment cash inflow + performance adjustments) ÷ preceding value; the total return is the product of factors minus one. At this investment boundary buys add capital and sells release it; same-day flows across holdings are netted. Dividends and interest are return, and fees are negative adjustments. This reduces the effect of contribution timing rather than treating new capital as a gain.

MWR — Money-Weighted Return

MWR solves an annualized XIRR from dated investor cash flows and the terminal investment value. Buys are negative, sells and investment income positive, fees negative and terminal value positive. Same-day flows are combined. The solver uses actual calendar days divided by 365: a short-period MWR is still annualized. Date ranges start with a negative opening market value. Base-currency cash remains outside this investment boundary; merely depositing money into that cash account is not an investment cash flow.

An XIRR can have multiple solutions or no valid solution. This implementation starts its solver at zero and can return zero when it cannot determine a valid root or when dated flows are insufficient. A displayed zero therefore does not always prove that the investment broke even. TWR/MWR percentage routines do not explicitly deduct the Tax field in this baseline; use the monetary net result and ROAI/ROI for reviewing recorded taxes.

Worked examples

No external investment flows: invest 1,000 EUR, keep the same invested capital for 365 days and end with 1,100 EUR, with no income, fees, taxes or currency effects. Profit is 100 EUR; ROI and ROAI are 10%, cumulative TWR is 10% and annualized MWR is 10%.

Additional contribution: invest 1,000 EUR on day 1 and another 1,000 on day 3 at unchanged prices; the final value on day 4 is 2,100 EUR. The extra money is invested, not just left in base-currency cash. Gain is 100, not 1,100. ROI uses 2,000 of capital → 5%. In the day-1-to-day-4 chart, ROAI uses (1,000 × 2 + 2,000 × 2) ÷ 4 = 1,500 → about 6.67%. The whole-history holding average uses the three elapsed days: (1,000 × 2 + 2,000 × 1) ÷ 3 ≈ 1,333.33 → 7.5%. Compare like-for-like displays. Assume no costs, income or currency effects.

Timing matters: invest 1,000 EUR, gain 20% over 365 days to reach 1,200, then invest another 1,000. Lose 10% over the next 365 days and finish at 1,980. TWR is 1.20 × 0.90 − 1 = +8% over both periods, about +3.92% annualized for comparison. MWR solves −1,000 − 1,000 ÷ (1 + r) + 1,980 ÷ (1 + r)² = 0 and is about −0.67% annually. More money was exposed to the losing period. The application’s cumulative TWR and annualized MWR have different time bases; compare them accordingly.

Benchmarks

In Analysis, choose an available benchmark in the comparator. Its line is the benchmark price change from its first available point in the portfolio range, adjusted for the benchmark currency’s change against your base currency. It is not recalculated with your deposits, fees, taxes or MWR timing, and no separate dividend reinvestment is added by the comparator. The market-data provider determines the supplied price series. For example, a price from 100 to 110 with unchanged currency gives a +10% benchmark.

The portfolio line uses your selected performance method; the benchmark remains a price-based comparison, not an identical investor experience. MWR is annualized while the benchmark price change is cumulative. Missing baseline prices or exchange data can limit the comparison. Check dates, currency, income and cost treatment before interpreting a gap as outperformance.
